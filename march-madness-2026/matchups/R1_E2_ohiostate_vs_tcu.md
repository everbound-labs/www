### Analysis & Prediction

This is a classic 8-vs-9 coin flip elevated by contrasting styles and genuine intrigue. Both teams enter on hot streaks after inconsistent seasons. The case for each side:

**The case for Ohio State:**
Ohio State's backcourt is the decisive advantage in this matchup. Bruce Thornton is playing at an All-American level, averaging 22.5 PPG over his last 10 games with elite efficiency, and John Mobley Jr. is one of the most dangerous shooters in the country at 41.8% from three. TCU simply does not have a backcourt answer for Thornton and Mobley. When both guards are shooting well, Ohio State's offensive ceiling is among the highest in the field — the Buckeyes' adjusted offensive efficiency ranks 35th nationally. The Buckeyes also have a significant size advantage with 7-0 Tilly and 7-2 Njegovan against TCU's 6-7/6-8 frontcourt, and the return of Noel and likely return of Chatman gives Ohio State its deepest rotation since early January.

Bart Torvik's model favoring Ohio State by 4.9 points is notable — that is not typical for an 8-9 game and suggests Ohio State is meaningfully underseeded. KenPom's adjusted efficiency margin also favors the Buckeyes. Ohio State's offense has been elite since February, ranking 13th nationally, and four of their six best offensive performances came in March. They also beat a top-10 Purdue team during the regular season.

**The case for TCU:**
TCU's defense is legitimate, ranking inside the top 25 in adjusted defensive efficiency (102.3 per 100 possessions) compared to Ohio State's woeful 174th-ranked defense (108.2). The Horned Frogs have a clear rebounding edge (36.2 RPG vs. 33.2) and the Punch-Edmonds frontcourt duo provides physical inside presence. Jamie Dixon has been to 16 NCAA Tournaments; Jake Diebler has been to zero as a head coach. TCU won 9 of its last 11 and beat quality opponents (Iowa State, Texas Tech) down the stretch. The Frogs also play in the stronger conference — the Big 12 sent more teams to the tournament than the Big Ten.

**Why I pick Ohio State:**
The guard play gap is too significant. Thornton is operating at a level few players in the country can match right now, and Mobley's shooting stretches defenses in ways TCU's backcourt cannot replicate. TCU's defense is better, but Ohio State's offensive firepower — especially from the perimeter — has the higher variance in a single-elimination format. The Buckeyes' size advantage in the frontcourt (Tilly and Njegovan at 7-0 and 7-2) neutralizes the Punch-Edmonds physical edge, and Ohio State's return to full health is well-timed. Torvik's 4.9-point projection and multiple analytics models consistently favoring the Buckeyes by meaningful margins give further confidence. The 78% confidence reflects that this is still an 8-9 game with a strong TCU defense capable of making things ugly, but Ohio State's elite guard tandem and superior offensive efficiency should prevail.
